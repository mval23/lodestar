Lodestar: a fixed point for everyday money

A private personal-finance tracker with a calm, Apple-style interface: warm Stone greys, generous space, and a single electric-blue accent that appears only where it matters.

NameLodestar
StyleBearing concept, Apple-style interface
ColorStone greys + Electric blue
SymbolAxes & Fix
Recommendation our strategic call Option an alternative to consider Assumption something we took as given Needs approval needs a decision or clearance

Decisions so far

  • Approved: the name Lodestar, the Bearing concept, the Axes & Fix logo, Stone as the grey base, Electric blue as the accent, an Apple-style interface, and English only.
  • Rejected: the name Constella, the first spruce, brass and harbor palette (felt like a dull office) and the four brighter palettes (Cobalt & Marigold, Graphite & Tangerine, Lagoon & Coral, Raspberry & Butter).
  • Scope: Lodestar is for individuals. Each person has their own private account, with no shared or household access and no invitations.

Working assumptions Assumption

  • This is a web-first product with a responsive mobile layout. Native iOS and macOS apps may follow, so the system mirrors Apple’s interface conventions.
  • “Apple-style” means following Apple’s design approach (clarity, restraint, depth, system type, one tint color). It does not mean using Apple’s brand, logos or icon artwork.
  • There are no existing brand assets, domains or trademarks, and the name has not been through legal clearance.
  • The first release has no bank sync. Everything here is direction; no final vector artwork has been produced.
01

Strategic foundation

The approved name, built out in full.

Approved name

Lodestar

Meaning. From Middle English lode (“way, course”) plus star: the star that shows the way. Traditionally this is Polaris, which navigators relied on because it stays still while the rest of the sky turns. In everyday English it also means a guiding principle. Associations: orientation, steadiness, a trusted reference point, keeping on course.

Brand idea
A fixed point in a moving picture. Money keeps moving. Lodestar is the steady reference that tells you where you are.
Positioning
For people who want to understand their money without handing it over, Lodestar is the private finance tracker that shows exactly where you stand and what to do next. Banking apps are built to sell products, and spreadsheets need constant upkeep. Lodestar is both precise and calm.
Brand promise
You will always know where you stand, and what a sensible next move looks like.
Pillars
Orientation: one accurate, current picture across every account.
Precision: every number can be traced, reconciled and explained.
Privacy: your data belongs to you, and only you can see it.
Personality
Steady · Exact · Candid · Unhurried · Encouraging
Emotional benefit
The relief of knowing where you are. Vague worry about money turns into calm confidence.
Functional benefit
Accounts, transactions, budgets, bills, goals and reports in one private, precise place, with CSV import and export so your data is never locked in.
Perception
“A serious tool that respects me. It’s clear, it’s private, and it doesn’t lecture.”
Brand story
Sailors crossing open water didn’t need to see the whole ocean. They needed one point that stayed still, so they could work out where they were and choose a heading. Personal finances feel like open water: balances change, bills arrive, plans drift. Lodestar is that steady point. It is a quiet, private tracker that shows where you stand today, so tomorrow’s decisions feel deliberate rather than anxious.

Naming rationale

Why Lodestar, and what still needs clearance. Scores are strategic judgments on a 1–5 scale, not research results.

The confusion-risk notes come from general knowledge, not a trademark search. A formal clearance search in every launch market, in classes 9, 36 and 42, is required before launch.

Approved

The name is Lodestar.

Why. The name is the brand promise. A lodestar is the point you check to know where you are before deciding where to go, and that is exactly what the product does. It carries built-in credibility without sounding like a bank. It also gives the identity a rich, abstract vocabulary (axes, bearings, a fixed point) that stays away from the forbidden star, space and crypto clichés.

Main tradeoff. Lodestar gains clear meaning and gravity but loses ownability. It is a dictionary word in a crowded namespace, including some crypto use, so domains and handles will probably need a modifier (for example lodestar.app or uselodestar). It can also read as serious, so the identity adds warmth through color, copy and motion.

03

Visual territories

The approved Lodestar direction, then three genuinely different territories that were explored for it.

The approved direction

Lodestar · “Bearing”

Instrument-like precision: an L-corner (the axes, your position) and one plotted point (the fixed reference you steer by). Warm Stone greys with one electric-blue spark, set in Apple-style system type.

Three territories for Lodestar

Territory A Recommended

Bearing: the precision of a navigational instrument, with an Apple-style interface

Concept
Borrowed from chart tables, compasses and sextants: tools built for one careful reading. Your position is plotted, never guessed. Why it works: it expresses precision and trust without looking like a bank, and it translates directly into data-dense product UI.
Mood
Quiet, exact, well-lit. A tidy desk at 9 a.m.
Logo idea
Axes & Fix: an L-shaped corner (the axes of your position, and the initial L) with one small square plotted in the open quadrant. That square is the fixed point you steer by. See Section 04.
Graphic language
Graduated tick rules, hairline axes, corner brackets that frame the key figure, and the blue “fix” square that marks now or you are here.
Color
Warm Stone greys do almost all the work. Electric blue is the single tint: it marks what you can tap or click, the fix, and “now.”
Typography
Apple’s system typeface (SF Pro) on Apple devices, with Inter elsewhere. Tabular figures throughout.
Icons
SF Symbols on Apple platforms, and a matched rounded-stroke set on the web.
Data viz
Thin 2 px lines, bars with a small 2 px top radius, recessive tick scales, direct labels, and a blue fix marker on the current value.
Imagery
Almost no illustration. When needed: close, daylight photography of real desks, paper and hands (no faces required), in a desaturated, cool-neutral grade.
Motion
Apple-style springs: quick and damped, never bouncy. The fix settles into place like a needle coming to rest.
Distinctive
The fix square and corner bracket are unique to this brand, yet they read as plain UI. The brand lives inside the product rather than sitting on top of it.
Risk
It can drift cold or technical. The Apple-style refinement counters this with warm Stone greys, generous space, soft materials and approachable empty states.
Territory B Option

Horizon: the line between now and next

Concept
The lodestar sits just above the horizon. The horizon line divides where you are (below) from where you are heading (above). Why it works: it puts the “quietly optimistic” quality first, with a forward-looking, human feel.
Mood
Open, editorial, early-morning calm.
Logo idea
A lowercase serif wordmark, with a small tilted square that sits above the baseline after the final “r,” like a point just clearing the horizon.
Graphic language
Wide horizontal two-tone fields split by one horizon line, with plenty of negative space. A single raised element per composition.
Color
Mist blue-grey sky, deep moss ground, and a clay accent. The warm-cool split carries the meaning.
Typography
A contemporary text serif for display (for example Newsreader or Tiempos), paired with a grotesk for UI.
Icons
Soft 1.75 px strokes with rounded ends.
Data viz
Area charts with a strong zero “horizon,” goals drawn above the line, and a baseline that is always visible.
Imagery
Real landscapes with low horizons, never staged lifestyle shots.
Motion
Slow vertical reveals, as if the horizon is lifting. 300–400 ms.
Distinctive
Very ownable in marketing, and emotionally warm.
Risk
A serif-led look can tip into luxury or wealth-management cues. Large fields do not suit dense tables, so the product and the marketing would diverge.
Territory C Option

Waypoint: a map of the route you’re on

Concept
Wayfinding and cartography. Goals are destinations, milestones are waypoints, and your history becomes terrain. Why it works: it makes progress tangible and suits the savings-goal and planning features.
Mood
Exploratory, tactile, energetic.
Logo idea
A condensed uppercase wordmark with a waypoint ring (a circle around a dot, the chart symbol for a fixed position) replacing the counter of the “O.”
Graphic language
Contour lines generated from the user’s own data (net-worth history drawn as terrain), dashed routes and waypoint rings.
Color
Chalk, graphite and lichen green, with a trail-marker orange signal.
Typography
A condensed grotesk for display (signage feel) and a mono for coordinates and figures.
Icons
Signage-style pictograms, filled, on a strict grid.
Data viz
Route-style progress lines, milestone markers, and elevation-profile area charts.
Imagery
Generative topographic textures rather than photography.
Motion
Route lines draw themselves, and markers drop in as you reach them.
Distinctive
Very memorable and feels good to use, with strong gamified goals.
Risk
It reads as outdoors or travel. Contour textures are a current trend and easy to overuse. It gets busy in dense views, and the orange collides with warning states.
04

Logo system: Axes & Fix

The symbol stays exactly as approved. Only its color changes: ink axes with an electric-blue fix. These are concept renderings, not finished vector artwork. Needs approval

Symbol construction

Symbol concept

Grid
A 16 × 16 unit square with a 2-unit margin, so the live area is 12 × 12. The same drawing is the favicon master, so every edge lands on a whole pixel at 16 px.
The axes
An L-shaped corner. The vertical arm runs from x 2–5 and y 2–14. The horizontal arm runs from x 2–14 and y 11–14. Stroke is 3 units, with square terminals and a sharp corner.
The fix
A solid 3 × 3 square at x 8–11, y 5–8, sitting in the open quadrant, 3 units from each arm, with its center on the 45° diagonal from the inner corner. It is the fixed point you steer by.
Color
Two colors: ink axes (#1C1A17) with an electric-blue fix (#1F4BFF; #6F8BFF on dark). One color: the whole mark in ink or white. Blue holds 6:1 contrast on white, so the two-color mark works even at 16 px.
Sharp on purpose
The interface is soft and rounded in Apple style, but the mark keeps its sharp corners. That contrast is what makes it feel precise.
Explore
Option Try the fix as a square rotated 45°, a quiet nod to a star point. Test it at 16 px before adopting.

Lockups & formats

Horizontal lockup (primary)

The symbol’s bottom arm sits on the wordmark baseline. Symbol height (the 12-unit live area) is 1.15× the cap height. The gap between symbol and wordmark is 4 units.

Compact lockup

Stacked version for splash screens, the sign-in screen and square spaces. The wordmark is centered below at 0.5× the symbol height.

App icon: light, dark, tinted

Light is the default: a Stone-white tile with a barely visible top-to-bottom gradient, ink axes and a blue fix. Dark and tinted versions follow Apple’s appearance options. Supply the tile, the axes and the fix as separate layers so the icon can be built in Apple’s Icon Composer. Needs approval Check against the current Apple HIG.

Favicon · 16 × 16

Two colors and pixel-snapped: 3 px strokes, 3 px gaps. The SVG favicon switches to Stone-white with a light-blue fix when the browser is in dark mode (via prefers-color-scheme).

Monochrome & reversed

One-color ink or Stone-white works anywhere. On dark grounds the fix uses light blue #6F8BFF, because the standard blue drops to 2.8:1 on ink.

Clear space & minimum size

X = the side of the fix square. Keep at least 2X clear on every side. Minimum sizes: symbol 16 px (use the favicon master below 24 px); horizontal lockup 88 px wide on screen, 22 mm in print; wordmark alone 64 px.

Wordmark concept

“Lodestar” in sentence case, in the spirit of Apple’s own typography: semibold, tightly spaced and quiet. Start from Inter Display SemiBold and redraw it. SF Pro’s license does not allow it in a logo. Required refinements: (1) the t crossbar extends to the right only, a small echo of the L-corner; (2) the L is kerned tight to the o; (3) the counters are opened slightly for small sizes; (4) tracking is −2% at display sizes and 0 below 20 px. No stars, dots or swashes: the symbol does that work.

Small-size behavior

  • ≤ 24 px: use the pixel-snapped 16-unit master. Never scale the full lockup down to this size.
  • All sizes: two colors are allowed on light grounds (blue on white is 6:1). On dark grounds, switch the fix to light blue.
  • The recognition test: at 16 px, the mark still reads as “a corner and a point” in a browser tab next to ten other favicons.

Incorrect use

✕ RotateThe fix always stays in the upper-right quadrant.
✕ MirrorA mirrored L reads as a different corner.
✕ Status colorsThe fix is never red or green; it doesn’t signal gains or losses.
✕ Round the cornersThe mark stays sharp even though the interface is rounded.
✕ Outline or glowNo outline, glass, glow or gradient on the mark itself.
✕ Literal starThe fix is never a star, sparkle or dot cluster.
✕ Dark blue on darkOnly 2.8:1 contrast. Use light blue #6F8BFF instead.
✕ StretchAlways scale in proportion.
✕ Move the fixKeep the 3-unit gap and the 45° diagonal.

Chart colors

Most charts use no color at all. Money in is ink and money out is Stone 500, so they are told apart by lightness. That works for colorblind users and in print. 06

Typography

Apple-style type: the system font, big confident titles with tight letter spacing, and quiet, readable body text. Numbers always use tabular figures.

Recommendation

System font: SF Pro, then Inter

On Apple devices the product uses the system font, SF Pro (Text below 20 px, Display at 20 px and above; the browser switches automatically). Everywhere else it falls back to Inter, an open-source font designed in the same spirit.

font-family: -apple-system, BlinkMacSystemFont, "Inter", "Segoe UI", system-ui, sans-serif;

Numbers: font-variant-numeric: tabular-nums on every amount, so columns line up. SF Pro and Inter both support it.

Mono: ui-monospace (SF Mono on Apple devices, Menlo or Consolas elsewhere), only for reference numbers, card masks and CSV previews.

Needs approval

Licensing note

SF Pro can be used in apps for Apple platforms, and it reaches the web through the system font stack. Never self-host SF font files on the web, and never use SF in the logo or marketing artwork. The wordmark is drawn from Inter Display instead.

Native apps

Use Apple’s Dynamic Type text styles directly (Large Title, Title 1–3, Headline, Body, Callout, Subheadline, Footnote, Caption), so text scales with each user’s accessibility settings.

Tables & forms

  • Table rows use Body 15/22. Headers use Caption: weight 500, secondary label color, sentence case.

07

Graphic language

Apple’s principles (clarity, deference to content, and depth) combined with Lodestar’s three instrument details.

Corner bracket

Thin blue corners with rounded line ends, around the one number that matters on a screen. At most one per view.

The fix & “now”

The blue square marks the current position, such as today’s balance or this month. A thin dashed blue line marks “now” on time charts. One per chart.

Graduated rule

Fine ticks under progress bars, so progress reads as a measurement rather than a game. The bars themselves are rounded capsules, in Apple style.

Layout & spacing
An 8 pt grid (4, 8, 12, 16, 20, 24, 32, 44, 64) with generous margins: 20 px on phones and 32 px or more on desktop. Desktop is a sidebar and content split, like macOS apps. Mobile uses a tab bar with large titles.
Corners
Continuous, squircle-style corners: 8 px on small controls, 12 px on fields and buttons, 16 px on grouped containers, and the platform’s mask on the app icon. Buttons can be capsules. Tables inside a group have no inner corners.
Materials & depth
Translucent “glass” (background blur plus saturation) only on navigation: the sidebar, toolbar and tab bar. Content sits on solid white groups against Stone. Soft, large shadows only on floating layers such as popovers, sheets and menus.
Containers
Apple-style inset grouped lists for settings, accounts and forms. Dashboards use a few large white groups, not a mosaic of small floating cards. Dense data such as transactions goes in one continuous table.
Iconography
SF Symbols in native apps. On the web, a matched rounded-stroke set (for example Lucide at 1.75 px). Icons are ink or grey, and blue only when selected. No coins, wallets, piggy banks or bank buildings.
Charts
In the style of Apple’s Swift Charts: bars with rounded tops, a very light horizontal grid, and axis labels in grey Footnote text. Hovering or dragging shows a vertical line with the exact value. A “Show as table” option is always available.
Tables
Hairline separators inset from the left edge, as in iOS lists. The selected row gets a blue tint. Headers stay fixed at the top while scrolling. Pending items appear in grey with a “Pending” capsule.
Imagery
Marketing uses real product screenshots on Stone, never stock photography. Any photos are soft, daylight and neutral in tone, with no luxury cues and no piles of cash.
Empty states
A large icon in light grey, a one-line title, one sentence, and one blue button. Calm, never cute.
Motion
Springs rather than fixed-length animations: quick (about 0.35 s) and damped, with no visible bounce. Sheets slide up, and numbers cross-fade instead of counting up. The fix does a gentle settle. When the user turns on Reduce Motion (prefers-reduced-motion), everything becomes a simple fade.

Voice & language

Lodestar sounds like a steady friend who is good with numbers: plain, specific, and never judging.

Brand voice

Steady, exact, candid, kind. We state what is true, show the number behind it, and suggest a next step without pressure. We sound the same on a good month and a hard one.

Tone principles

  • Facts before feelings. “Spending is $38 over plan,” not “Uh-oh!”
  • Neutral about choices. Spending is never “bad.” Plans change, and we just show where things stand.
  • Specific over clever. Name the account, the amount and the date.
  • One metaphor, used rarely. Navigation language (“bearings,” “on course”) is for marketing moments, never for functional labels.
  • Private means quiet. No growth-hacking nudges, streaks or social comparison.

Five rules that make it recognizable

  1. Monochrome first. Stone, white and ink carry about 95% of every screen.
  2. Blue means act or now. It marks what you can tap or click, the fix, and the current position. Nothing else.
  3. One bracket, one fix. The corner bracket frames the one number that matters; the blue square marks where you are.
  4. Soft interface, sharp mark. The UI has rounded corners and glass navigation, while the logo stays crisp and square.
  5. Numbers set like an instrument: tabular, with true minus signs, aligned, and bigger than their labels.

Five rules that protect consistency

  1. Every color, size, radius and spring comes from tokens. No one-off hex values in product code.
  2. Status colors appear only for real states, always with an icon and words, and never as chart series or decoration.
  3. Glass is only for navigation (sidebar, toolbar, tab bar), never for content.
  4. Charts are grey by default. Category colors are allowed only in breakdowns, in their fixed order and with labels.
  5. Follow Apple’s interface conventions, but never copy Apple’s brand, icons or artwork. On the web, use SF only through the system font stack.

Accessibility considerations

  • Target WCAG 2.2 AA. Text is at least 4.5:1 (every listed pair passes). Control borders and graphics are at least 3:1 (inputs use Stone 500 or darker). Focus rings are visible and use a translucent blue halo.
  • Nothing relies on color alone: signs, icons, labels, hatching and lightness carry gains, losses, overages and errors.
  • Glass surfaces keep text at least 4.5:1 over any content behind them. They become solid when the user turns on “Reduce transparency.”
  • Touch targets are at least 44 × 44 pt (Apple) and 24 × 24 px on desktop web (WCAG 2.5.8). Native apps support Dynamic Type; the web version supports 200% zoom and reflows at 320 px wide.
  • Respect Reduce Motion, dark mode and Increase Contrast. Dark mode uses its own checked colors, not an automatic inversion.
  • Screen readers announce amounts in full (“minus 64 dollars and 20 cents”) and card masks as “card ending 4821.”
